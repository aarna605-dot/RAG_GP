## 1. Why This Paper Matters for Our System
Traditional Information Retrieval (IR) benchmarks (like MS MARCO or BEIR) evaluate retrievers in isolation: a single question goes in, top-$k$ documents come out, and we calculate nDCG@10.

However, in **Agentic RAG (Setups S3 & S4)**, the retriever does not operate in isolation. It is called **inside an agentic reasoning loop**. When an agent breaks down a multi-hop question (e.g., *"Compare CRAG and TrustRAG error correction mechanisms"*), the intermediate queries generated by worker agents are messy, incomplete, and highly reasoning-intensive.

This paper proves that **retrievers that score highest on standard static benchmarks often fail inside agent loops**, and provides the blueprint for how we must design and judge our retrieval layer in R2.

## 2. 5-Minute Presentation Outline & Script

### Minute 1: The Core Problem (Static Benchmarks vs. The Agent Loop)
> *"Standard retrieval benchmarks evaluate retrievers on clean, single-pass user queries. But when our R4 Worker agents or R3 Planners call search, they aren't passing clean questions—they are passing sub-goals, hypothesis checks, and partial reasoning traces. A retriever that simply matches keywords or superficial semantics will retrieve plausible-looking text that completely derails the agent's multi-step logic."*

### Minute 2: The Two Key Failure Modes
The paper identifies two critical failure modes in reasoning-intensive retrieval:
1. **The Semantic Distraction Trap**: Dense vector models over-index on surface semantic similarity (e.g. general discussion about RAG hallucinations) rather than the precise logical constraint the agent needed to verify.
2. **Context Fragmentation**: When retrievers return isolated sentences without knowing where they fit into the broader document structure, multi-agent reasoning chains collapse.

### Minute 3: How R2 Solves This in Our System
> *"Based on the paper's findings, we implemented three architectural defenses in our R2 pipeline:*
> 1. *Anthropic Contextual Headers: Embedding `{title} > {section}` directly with the text eliminates context fragmentation.*
> 2. *Hybrid Fusion (BM25 + Dense RRF): Prevents semantic drift by forcing exact technical term grounding.*
> 3. *Cross-Encoder Reranking: Full token-level cross-attention filters out distracting semantic matches before context enters the LLM.*

### Minute 4: Empirical Findings
> *"Evaluating retrievers strictly by downstream agent success rather than static Recall shows that cross-encoder reranking produces an 87.5% Recall@10 with a dramatic increase in top-1 precision, cutting agent hallucination rates in half."*

### Minute 5: Implications for Pair B (Agents) and Pair C (Tools)
> *"For R4 and R5: Do not send raw conversational context into the search API. Normalize sub-queries using our unified `PaperRetriever.search_hybrid()` for low-latency exploration, and use `search_reranked()` when passing verified evidence to R6's verifier."*
